Research note: the main limit was access. congress.gov, Vicente LLP and several Brazilian news sites returned 403, and Firecrawl ran out of credits, so I did not read the enacted bill text directly. The statutory details below come from law-firm and CRS summaries that agree with each other (Frier Levitt, Foley Hoag, Hemp Law Group, Venable, CRS via search snippets). ## PART 1, Q1: What exactly did the November 2025 law change, and when does it take effect?

### Takeaway
Section 781 of Division B of P.L. 119-37 (H.R. 5371, the CR/Agriculture appropriations package signed 12 Nov 2025 that ended the shutdown) redefines "hemp". It moves to a total-THC standard (0.3% dry weight, counting THCA). It caps final hemp-derived cannabinoid products at 0.4 mg total THC per container, and it excludes synthesized or non-naturally-occurring cannabinoids. The original effective date was 12 Nov 2026. A stopgap funding law signed 2 Sep 2026 pushed most of it to **11 December 2026**. Synthetic and non-naturally-occurring cannabinoids still lose hemp status on **12 Nov 2026**.

### Cited Findings
- Section 781, Division B of P.L. 119-37 (H.R. 5371) was signed 12 Nov 2025 and amends the hemp definition in 7 U.S.C. §1639o. — [Cannabis Business Times / CRS via search](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF13136); [Mondaq / FDA Law Blog](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2026/01/changes-to-hemp-definition-set-to-close-the-farm-bill-loophole/)
- Hemp is now measured by **total THC** (including THCA and delta-8) at not more than 0.3% on a dry-weight basis. — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/federal-hemp-redefinition-2026-thc-limits-compliance/); [Hemp Law Group](https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand)
- Final hemp-derived cannabinoid products are capped at **0.4 mg total THC per container** (plus cannabinoids with similar effects), measured per container and not per serving. — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/federal-hemp-redefinition-2026-thc-limits-compliance/); [Hemp Law Group](https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand)
- Products with cannabinoids "synthesized or manufactured outside the plant", or not "naturally produced by the cannabis plant" (example: HHC), are excluded from hemp. — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/federal-hemp-redefinition-2026-thc-limits-compliance/)
- The statute defines "container" as the innermost wrapping or vessel in direct contact with the product. — [Hemp Law Group](https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand)
- FDA was told to publish, within 90 days of enactment, lists of naturally occurring cannabinoids, THC-class cannabinoids and cannabinoids with similar effects, and to add detail on "container". As of 19 Oct 2026 these were **still unpublished**. — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/federal-hemp-redefinition-2026-thc-limits-compliance/); [Hemp Law Group](https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand)
- Products outside the new definition become "marijuana" under the Controlled Substances Act. — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/federal-hemp-redefinition-2026-thc-limits-compliance/)
- Industrial hemp (fiber, grain, oil, seeds, microgreens, research) is carved out and not targeted. — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/federal-hemp-redefinition-2026-thc-limits-compliance/); [Hemp Law Group](https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand)
- **Delay:** H.R. 6500 (Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027) was signed by President Trump on 2 Sep 2026. It moved the restrictions on naturally derived hemp-THC products from 12 Nov 2026 to **11 Dec 2026**. Votes: Senate 90-6 after a Budd amendment to strip the delay was tabled 61-32; House 370-48 on 1 Sep. — [Foley Hoag](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/cannabis-and-the-law/2026/september/a-temporary-reprieve-for-hemp-thc-products%E2%80%A6-but-what-now/); [Vicente LLP (search snippet)](https://vicentellp.com/insights/federal-hemp-definition-change-delay/); [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-features/2026/10/19/889119.htm)
- CRS cites the delaying law as **P.L. 119-103** (2 Sep 2026), delaying "portions" of the new definition to 11 Dec 2026. — [CRS IF13136 (search snippet)](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF13136/IF13136.5.pdf)
- **Not delayed:** fully synthetic cannabinoids ("not capable of being naturally produced by a Cannabis sativa L. plant") still lose hemp status on 12 Nov 2026. — [Foley Hoag](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/cannabis-and-the-law/2026/september/a-temporary-reprieve-for-hemp-thc-products%E2%80%A6-but-what-now/); [Hemp Law Group](https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand)
- Until those dates the 2018 Farm Bill definition (delta-9 THC ≤0.3%) remains the operative federal standard. — [Hemp Law Group](https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand)
- Some states set their own dates: Illinois applies the 0.4 mg/container rule from 12 Nov 2026 regardless of the federal delay. — [Illinois Dept. of Revenue FY 2027-04](https://tax.illinois.gov/research/publications/bulletins/fy-2027-04.html)

### Inferences
- In practice, full-spectrum products in the US face two deadlines: 12 Nov 2026 (synthetics only, which does not affect a natural full-spectrum CBD oil) and 11 Dec 2026, when the total-THC and 0.4 mg/container rules apply.
- Several older pages still say "12 November". Any report should use 11 Dec 2026 for the 0.4 mg rule and cite H.R. 6500 / P.L. 119-103.
- From my reading of the statute (not verified against the bill text here), the definition also excludes intermediate hemp-derived cannabinoid products marketed or sold as final products or directly to consumers. Bulk crude or distillate sold B2B is judged by the 0.3% total-THC dry-weight test, not the 0.4 mg cap. Verify at congress.gov.

### Gaps
- I could not read the enrolled text of Sec. 781 or H.R. 6500 (congress.gov returned 403). The exact wording of the "hemp-derived cannabinoid products" definition and the delay clause is unverified.
- Whether FDA will publish the cannabinoid lists or "container" guidance before 11 Dec 2026 is unknown.

## PART 1, Q2: What happened in 2026 (delays, bills, FDA, lawsuits), and where do things stand in October 2026?

### Takeaway
The only delay enacted is the one-month move to 11 Dec 2026. Longer delays, repeals and regulatory frameworks are pending but none had cleared committee as of 19 Oct 2026. The White House reportedly said there would be no further extensions. Litigation has targeted state laws, not Sec. 781 itself.

### Cited Findings
- **H.R. 7024 / S. 3686, Hemp Planting Predictability Act** (introduced 13 Jan 2026; Baird) would replace "365 days" with "3 years", moving the effective date to 12 Nov 2028. Not enacted. — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/federal-hemp-redefinition-2026-thc-limits-compliance/); [Hemp Law Group](https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand)
- **H.R. 6209, American Hemp Protection Act** (Mace, 17 Nov 2025) would strike Sec. 781 and restore the 2018 definition. — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/federal-hemp-redefinition-2026-thc-limits-compliance/)
- **H.R. 9830, Lawful Hemp Protection Act** would regulate consumable hemp: 21+, a synthetics ban, a federal tax, and reportedly a 1% total-THC dry-weight limit. A Senate companion (Sheehy/Klobuchar) is expected but not filed. **H.R. 10079, Beverage Regulatory Parity Act** covers THC beverages (5 mg/serving). None had advanced past committee as of 19 Oct 2026. — [Hemp Law Group](https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand)
- The Cannabinoid Safety and Regulation Act (Wyden/Merkley, Dec 2025) proposes 5 mg/serving and 50 mg/container for edibles. — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/federal-hemp-redefinition-2026-thc-limits-compliance/)
- **2026 Farm Bill:** the House passed it 30 Apr 2026 without delaying or repealing the ban, after the Baird two-year delay amendment was not included. The Senate Ag Committee draft also leaves the ban in place, and its 6 Aug markup failed 10-11. The 2018 Farm Bill extension expired 30 Sep 2026. — [Hemp Law Group](https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand); [Cannabis Business Times](https://www.cannabisbusinesstimes.com/hemp/news/15823852/s-house-passes-2026-farm-bill-intoxicating-hemp-product-ban-remains)
- White House Legislative Affairs Director James Braid reportedly said there would be no extensions beyond 11 Dec. 35 state and territory AGs urged Congress not to reverse the ban (letter dated 4 Aug). — [Foley Hoag](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/cannabis-and-the-law/2026/september/a-temporary-reprieve-for-hemp-thc-products%E2%80%A6-but-what-now/); [Hemp Law Group](https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand)
- **Executive Order of 18 Dec 2025** ("Increasing Medical Marijuana and Cannabidiol Research"): tells the AG to finish Schedule III rulemaking and tells White House staff to "work with the Congress to update the statutory definition of final hemp-derived cannabinoid products", including per-serving and per-container limits and CBD:THC ratios. It acknowledges some full-spectrum CBD products will become controlled marijuana under Sec. 781. It does not change Sec. 781's timeline. — [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/01/executive-order-on-medical-marijuana-and-cbd)
- The administration launched a Medicare initiative covering up to $500/year in hemp-derived products for eligible patients, and the President urged Congress to fix the law for full-spectrum CBD. — [Foley Hoag](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/cannabis-and-the-law/2026/september/a-temporary-reprieve-for-hemp-thc-products%E2%80%A6-but-what-now/)
- **Lawsuits** target state laws, not Sec. 781: Ohio SB 56 (a federal PI covering 10 companies), Texas (state suit plus an Aug 2026 federal Commerce Clause case), California emergency rules (TRO denied), Alabama (TRO denied), Tennessee (Cornbread Hemp). — [Foley Hoag](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/cannabis-and-the-law/2026/september/a-temporary-reprieve-for-hemp-thc-products%E2%80%A6-but-what-now/)
- CRS says it is unclear whether and how federal agencies will enforce the new prohibitions, and no enforcement plan has been detailed. — [Hemp Law Group](https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand)
- Square told merchants to remove CBD and hemp products by 15 Oct 2026. — [Hemp Law Group](https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand); [Cannabis Legalization News](https://cannabislegalizationnews.com/square-cbd-hemp-ban-october/)

### Inferences
- The base case for planning is that the 0.4 mg/container rule takes effect 11 Dec 2026. A fix for full-spectrum CBD is plausible given the White House signals, but nothing has been enacted and the time before 11 Dec is short. Plan for supply continuity on both scenarios.
- I found no federal lawsuit directly challenging Sec. 781 as of October 2026.

### Gaps
- Whether a lame-duck vehicle (for example, the CR expiring around 11 Dec or an omnibus) will carry another delay or a CBD carve-out is unknown.
- I found no DOJ, DEA or FDA enforcement guidance.

## PART 1, Q3: Does the change affect products made for export? Are full-spectrum 30 ml tinctures illegal to produce and ship? What about broad spectrum and isolate?

### Takeaway
None of the sources I found mention an export exemption in Sec. 781. A full-spectrum tincture with more than 0.4 mg total THC per container will be "marijuana" under the CSA from 11 Dec 2026, and that status does not depend on where the product is going. Broad spectrum and isolate with non-detectable THC generally remain hemp.

### Cited Findings
- Industry estimates say 90% or more of non-intoxicating full-spectrum CBD products would fall outside the amended definition because of the 0.4 mg cap. The U.S. Hemp Roundtable figure is more than 90%. — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/federal-hemp-redefinition-2026-thc-limits-compliance/); [ProCannabis / industry](https://procannabis.com/blog-1/hemp-ban-2025-explained-will-thca-delta-and-cbd-be-illegal/)
- The 18 Dec 2025 EO itself acknowledges that some full-spectrum CBD products "will again be treated as controlled marijuana" under the CSA when Sec. 781 takes effect. — [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/01/executive-order-on-medical-marijuana-and-cbd)
- Isolate and broad-spectrum CBD are generally expected to stay within the cap because their THC is typically undetectable. Source quality is low (trade groups and retailers). — [search summary of industry sources](https://justkana.com/blogs/cbd/hemp-ban-2025)
- Hemp Law Group, CRS and Frier Levitt do not address manufacturing for export or treat export as a separate category. — [Hemp Law Group](https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand); [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/federal-hemp-redefinition-2026-thc-limits-compliance/)
- US demand for extracts is expected to shift to South American extractors (Uruguay, Colombia, Argentina). — [MercoPress, 14 Oct 2026](https://en.mercopress.com/2026/10/14/new-us-hemp-rules-take-effect-december-11-shifting-demand-for-south-american-exporters)

### Inferences
- **Arithmetic:** a 30 ml full-spectrum oil at about 0.2% THC by weight holds about 50 mg THC, which is far above 0.4 mg. Even a "<0.3%" 1,000 mg CBD bottle would exceed the cap by roughly two orders of magnitude. Full-spectrum 30 ml tinctures will almost certainly be non-hemp after 11 Dec 2026. This is my own arithmetic, not a cited figure.
- **Export:** once a product is "marijuana" under the CSA, making, holding and shipping it are CSA-regulated activities wherever the buyer is. Exporting Schedule I substances requires DEA exporter registration and permits (21 U.S.C. §953). Marijuana's April 2026 partial move to Schedule III covers only FDA-approved products and state-licensed medical programs, not hemp-brand e-commerce exports. A US brand could not lawfully export full-spectrum >0.4 mg bottles to Brazil without DEA registration, which is unrealistic. This is legal reasoning, not a cited source. **Confirm with US counsel.**
- **Brazil-side knock-on:** Anvisa requires the product to be manufactured or distributed by establishments regularized in the country of origin. A product that becomes federally "marijuana" in the US could fail that test even if it legally crosses the border. — basis: [Cannabis & Saúde](https://www.cannabisesaude.com.br/como-importar-canabidiol-pela-anvisa/) and RDC 570/2021 summary at [Bahia Notícias](https://www.bahianoticias.com.br/saude/noticia/27784-avisa-altera-norma-para-otimizar-importacao-de-produtos-de-cannabis). The inference is mine.
- **Options:**
  - (a) Move the SKU mix to broad spectrum and isolate (non-detect THC).
  - (b) Use smaller containers so each "container" has ≤0.4 mg. This is impractical for oils: even 1 ml at 0.2% THC is about 2 mg.
  - (c) Source full-spectrum from Colombia, Uruguay, Canada or the EU.
  - (d) Use US full-spectrum inventory made and sold before 11 Dec 2026, which still carries risk for stock held after that date.
- Bulk intermediates (crude or distillate ≤0.3% total THC dry weight) may remain hemp B2B, but they are not a finished-product export solution.

### Gaps
- I found no law-firm analysis that addresses export under Sec. 781. Treat this as an open legal question for US cannabis counsel.
- Whether DEA or CBP will actively police outbound low-value parcels is unknown.

## PART 1, Q4: Marijuana rescheduling to Schedule III. Status, and does it matter for exports to Brazil?

### Takeaway
Rescheduling is partial. A final order (about April 2026) moved FDA-approved marijuana products and marijuana within state-licensed medical programs to Schedule III. Broader rescheduling went to a DEA ALJ hearing (29 Jun–15 Jul 2026) with no final rule as of October 2026. It does not cover hemp-brand CBD export.

### Cited Findings
- The Acting AG (Todd Blanche) signed a final order moving FDA-approved marijuana products and state-licensed medical marijuana to Schedule III. Sources cite publication around 28 Apr 2026. Adult-use marijuana remains Schedule I. — [Saul Ewing](https://www.saul.com/insights/alert/dea-reschedules-fda-approved-marijuana-products-and-state-licensed-medical-marijuana); [Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/relief_finally_dea_issues_order_expediting_cannabis_rescheduling_schedule_iii_0426.html)
- The DEA ALJ hearing (Chief ALJ Derek Julius) ran 29 Jun–15 Jul 2026. The recommended decision is pending, and opponents sought more briefing and discovery. There is no final date. — [Morgan Lewis, Sep 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/09/high-time-for-a-change-post-dea-hearing-broader-marijuana-rescheduling-questions-remain); [Marijuana Herald, Sep 2026](https://themarijuanaherald.com/2026/09/whats-going-on-with-marijuana-rescheduling-where-the-federal-process-stands-and-what-happens-next/)
- The Dec 2025 EO directed the AG to finish Schedule III rulemaking expeditiously. — [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/01/executive-order-on-medical-marijuana-and-cbd)

### Inferences
- Even full Schedule III would keep marijuana a controlled substance. Export would still need DEA registration and export declarations or permits plus importing-country authorization. Rescheduling does not create a path for a small e-commerce brand to export full-spectrum >0.4 mg products, so it is largely irrelevant to Model A in the near term.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the exact Federal Register citation of the April 2026 order.

## PART 1, Q5: What are the export rules for hemp CBD from the US (licenses, certificates, carriers)?

### Takeaway
Compliant hemp CBD (≤0.4 mg/container after 11 Dec) has no specific US export license regime in the sources found. The binding constraints are carrier policy and destination-country rules. UPS and USPS prohibit international shipment of hemp cannabinoid products. FedEx and DHL policies are unclear or conflicting in the sources. Brazilian practice relies on express couriers.

### Cited Findings
- UPS "strictly prohibits international shipment of Hemp Cannabinoid finished products". USPS expressly prohibits international mailing of hemp cannabinoid products. Source is a 2021-era law-firm post. — [Harris Sliwoski](https://harris-sliwoski.com/cannalawblog/mailing-hemp-cannabinoid-products-in-the-u-s/)
- FedEx: one source says it allows hemp cannabinoid finished products ≤0.3% total THC, while another calls FedEx the most restrictive carrier. These conflict. — [Harris Sliwoski](https://harris-sliwoski.com/cannalawblog/mailing-hemp-cannabinoid-products-in-the-u-s/); [ShipBob](https://www.shipbob.com/blog/cbd-shipping/)
- DHL: a guide says DHL ships CBD with ≤0.3% THC and records, but it does not address international lanes. — [BigCommerce](https://www.bigcommerce.com/articles/selling-cbd-online/shipping-cbd/)
- Under RDC 660, only courier companies may make express international shipments containing CBD into Brazil. After nationalization and Anvisa release, the courier can hand off to Correios for the last mile. — [Sechat, Jun 2025](https://sechat.com.br/noticia/e-permitido-transportar-produtos-a-base-de-cannabis-pelos-correios)
- Certificates of Free Sale: the Florida Chamber does not issue them for ingestible or edible CBD and points to FDA. FDA issues export certificates for drugs, devices, supplements and so on, but is not required to for foods and cosmetics. Fee up to $175 for drugs and devices. — [Florida Chamber](https://www.flchamber.com/certificate-of-free-sale/?amp=1); [FindLaw summary of FDA requirements](https://corporate.findlaw.com/litigation-disputes/submission-requirements-for-requesting-certificates-for-exporting.html)

### Inferences
- An FDA Certificate to Foreign Government for CBD is unlikely. FDA holds that CBD cannot be a dietary supplement or food ingredient, so a CFS is effectively unavailable for unapproved CBD products. Brazil's RDC 660 personal import does not require a CFS. Brazilian intermediaries rely on manufacturer documents: CoA, manufacturer licenses, and the Anvisa authorization of the patient. This is inference, not cited.
- No USDA phytosanitary certificate is normally needed for processed extracts (an inference; phytosanitary rules cover plant material and seeds).
- An EAR/BIS export license is not normally required for hemp CBD at EAR99. This is an inference; I found no source. Low-value shipments under $2,500 per Schedule B line also generally don't need an EEI filing.
- **Carrier risk is the main practical risk.** Brazilian intermediaries typically use specialized courier consolidators (Courier Brasil Express and similar) operating under remessa expressa, not mainstream UPS or USPS international.

### Gaps
- I found no current 2026 FedEx or DHL international CBD policy page. Each must be confirmed directly, ideally in a written account agreement.

## PART 2, Q1: How do Brazilian intermediaries ship, what does it cost, how long does it take, how does clearance work, how is it taxed, and what goes wrong?

### Takeaway
Shipments enter Brazil by remessa expressa through couriers, not Correios international post. Anvisa sanitary inspection happens at clearance, against the patient's RDC 660 authorization. Receita Federal applies a 0% Import Tax to medicines imported by individuals up to US$10,000. ICMS is a state matter and charged separately. Delays at Anvisa and Receita are the most common problem.

### Cited Findings
- RDC 660/2022 allows individuals to import industrialized Cannabis-derived products for their own treatment with a prescription, after Anvisa approves the registration. The authorization is valid for two years and covers multiple imports. Prescriptions are valid for 6 months. — [Cannabis & Saúde](https://www.cannabisesaude.com.br/como-importar-canabidiol-pela-anvisa/); [Courier Brasil Express blog](https://www.courierbrasilexpress.com.br/blog/guia-completo-para-importacao-de-cbd-no-brasil)
- Anvisa opened a process in Jan 2026 to study updating the rule. — [Cannabis & Saúde](https://www.cannabisesaude.com.br/como-importar-canabidiol-pela-anvisa/)
- Anvisa response time: one source says up to 20 calendar days, another says about 10 days on average. — [Courier Brasil Express](https://www.courierbrasilexpress.com.br/blog/guia-completo-para-importacao-de-cbd-no-brasil); [Remessa Online](https://www.remessaonline.com.br/blog/como-importar-oleo-de-canabidiol-cbd/)
- Couriers report average delivery of about 10 days, depending on location. Products travel at controlled ambient temperature (15–30 °C). Transport requires firms with Anvisa Special Authorization (Portaria 344/1998, RDC 16/2014). — [Sechat](https://sechat.com.br/noticia/e-permitido-transportar-produtos-a-base-de-cannabis-pelos-correios)
- One supplier says the full process can take up to 30 days after authorization. — [CBMed](https://cbmed.com.br/artigos/o-que-e-a-rdc-660)
- 601 companies were registered under RDC 660 in 2024. 315,000 of 672,000 medical cannabis patients (47%) use RDC 660 imports. — [Sechat](https://sechat.com.br/noticia/e-permitido-transportar-produtos-a-base-de-cannabis-pelos-correios)
- **Receita Federal (official):** medicines imported by individuals via postal or express for own or individual use up to US$10,000 have a 0% Import Tax. Products with controlled substances (Portaria 344/98 Annex I) must follow Anvisa's personal-import route. Supplements and cosmetics are taxed normally. — [Receita Federal, "Como importar medicamentos?"](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/aduana-e-comercio-exterior/manuais/remessas-postal-e-expressa/como-importar-medicamentos)
- A 2017 ANAHP guide lists CBD products (including CBD+THC) as admitted, with II, PIS, COFINS and IPI at zero for express shipments by individuals up to US$10k. Postal shipment of such products is prohibited. — [ANAHP/HSL cartilha (2017)](https://guiafarmaceutico.hsl.org.br/informacoes-de-apoio/Documents/11.2017%20l%20CARTILHA%20DE%20IMPORTA%C3%87%C3%83O%20DE%20MEDICAMENTOS%20l%20GTLR%20ANAHP.pdf)
- History: the 2024 "taxa das blusinhas" law (PL 914/2024) initially dropped the medicine exemption. The government promised a fix, done by MP (one extended zero rate ran to 31 Mar 2025), and Receita's page now states the 0% rule. — [Poder360, 26 Jun 2024](https://www.poder360.com.br/governo/pl-das-comprinhas-nao-vai-retirar-isencao-de-remedios-importados/); [ABICANN](https://abicann.org/en/cannabis-medicinal-governo-afirma-que-importacao-direta-de-medicamentos-por-pacientes-continua-a-ter-isencao/)
- Lei 15.502/2026 (10 Sep 2026, reported by DHL) sets 0% II for certain medicines without a national equivalent, imported by individuals to treat rare or neglected diseases, with regulation due within 180 days. Not verified against the DOU. — [DHL Discover](https://www.dhl.com/discover/pt-br/assuntos-regulatorios/isencao-de-imposto-de-importacao); [Biored Brasil](https://www.bioredbrasil.com.br/sancionada-lei-que-preve-imposto-de-importacao-zero-para-medicamentos-sem-similar-nacional-destinados-a-doencas-raras/)
- ICMS on remittances is charged under each state's law, at 17–20%, collected by Correios or couriers (Remessa Conforme context). Receita's medicine page does not mention ICMS. — [Receita Federal Q&A](https://receita.economia.gov.br/orientacao/aduaneira/manuais/remessas-postal-e-expressa/topicos/Perguntas_e_Respostas)
- **Common problems:**
  - Jan 2026 reports of instability, with 300+ shipments stuck at Anvisa without analysis. — [Sechat EN](https://sechat.com.br/en/noticia/importation-flow-of-medicinal-cannabis-faces-delays-in-brazil)

  - Anvisa enforcement against sellers: in Nov 2025 it banned CBD Brasil–linked lots (RE 4.409/2025). — [Sechat](https://sechat.com.br/noticia/anvisa-determina-retirada-de-produtos-de-cannabis-da-cbd-brasil-do-mercado)
- If a medicine import is returned, the patient can file through Fala.BR with the tracking number and prescription. This is not possible once the item has gone back abroad. — [Receita Federal](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/aduana-e-comercio-exterior/manuais/remessas-postal-e-expressa/como-importar-medicamentos)

### Inferences
- Budget for II = 0, while treating ICMS as state-dependent and uncertain. Some states may exempt medicines and others may charge 17–20%. Get written confirmation from the chosen courier on how it collects ICMS for CBD medicines.
- Plan for 10–30 days door to door, and build buffer stock or reorder reminders for patients.

### Gaps

- I found no authoritative state-by-state ICMS table for CBD medicine imports.
- The exact current legal instrument for the 0% II (Portaria or MP) was not identified. Receita's page states the rule but cites no instrument.

## PART 2, Q3: How do payments flow (who receives BRL, processors, IOF, Banco Central eFX rules)?

### Takeaway
Mainstream processors exclude CBD: Stripe outright, and Square from 15 Oct 2026. High-risk processors are the fallback. On the Brazilian side, foreign merchants receive BRL from Brazilian consumers through eFX providers. From 1 Oct 2026, eFX can be offered only by BCB-authorized institutions, with a US$10k cap on non-integrated digital purchases. International card purchases carry 3.5% IOF.

### Cited Findings
- Stripe restricts "any products with CBD or THC in them" and cannot support hemp CBD sales even where legal. Its list also bars cannabis products and dispensaries. — [Stripe FAQ](https://support.stripe.com/questions/prohibited-and-restricted-business-list-faqs); [Stripe restricted businesses](https://stripe.com/us/restricted-businesses/)
- Square cut off CBD and hemp merchants on 15 Oct 2026. — [Hemp Law Group](https://www.hemplawgroup.com/news/federal-hemp-ban-2026-where-things-stand); [Cannabis Legalization News](https://cannabislegalizationnews.com/square-cbd-hemp-ban-october/)
- High-risk processors are the alternative, with higher fees, rolling reserves, and underwriting that takes weeks. Vendors recommend a backup processor. — [KORONA POS](https://koronapos.com/blog/square-cbd-requirements/); [PaymentCloud](https://paymentcloudinc.com/blog/square-cbd/)
- **eFX (BCB):** Resolução BCB 561 (30 Apr 2026) amends Resolução BCB 277. From 1 Oct 2026 only BCB-authorized institutions may provide eFX (one source cites an authorization deadline of 31 May 2027). Digital-payment acquisitions without e-commerce platform integration are capped at US$10,000. — [Legisweb: Res. BCB 561](https://www.legisweb.com.br/legislacao/?id=495246); [Machado Meyer](https://www.machadomeyer.com.br/pt/inteligencia-juridica/publicacoes-ij/bancario-seguros-e-financeiro-ij/banco-central-altera-regras-do-efx)
- **IOF:** 3.5% on international purchases by credit, debit and prepaid card since about 17 Jul 2025 (Decreto 12.499/2025), replacing the earlier phase-down to zero by 2028. Source is a payments company. Whether the 2025 congressional override affected this rate is unclear in the sources. — [ARQ Finance](https://arqfinance.com/articles/?p=2031); [Folha PE](https://www.folhape.com.br/economia/como-ficou-o-iof-de-cartao-de-credito-contas-internacionais-e/420811/)
- Remessa Online publishes a guide on paying for imported CBD, indicating international transfer is a common route. — [Remessa Online](https://www.remessaonline.com.br/blog/como-importar-oleo-de-canabidiol-cbd/)

### Inferences
- Two common flow models:
  - (A) The patient pays BRL via PIX or card to an eFX provider (dLocal, EBANX, PagBrasil types). The provider settles USD to the US seller, and IOF on the FX applies.
  - (B) A Brazilian intermediary company collects BRL and remits abroad via câmbio as an import of goods.
- In model B, the Brazilian entity's role must be consistent with RDC 660. The import is the patient's, and the intermediary acts as the patient's representative or agent.
- dLocal and EBANX must be asked directly whether they underwrite CBD. Given the BCB authorization requirement from Oct 2026, use only authorized eFX institutions.

### Gaps
- No sources on dLocal, EBANX, Payoneer or PagBrasil CBD policies were found.
- I could not confirm the IOF rate for eFX/PIX international payments (as distinct from cards), or the exact current IOF decree, against Planalto.

## PART 2, Q4: Should the founders open a US LLC (state, cost, banking, US tax, Brazilian tax)?

### Takeaway
A foreign-owned single-member LLC is a disregarded entity for US income tax but must file Form 5472 plus a pro-forma 1120, with a $25,000 penalty per form per year for failure. US tax applies only if the income is ECI. In Brazil, Lei 14.754/2023 taxes the profits of foreign controlled entities annually at 15% for resident individuals. Receita's Solução de Consulta Cosit 56/2026 treats transparent US LLCs with non-US-resident members as a privileged tax regime (regime fiscal privilegiado), which triggers extra burdens.

### Cited Findings
- Foreign-owned SMLLCs are treated as corporations for Form 5472 purposes. The $25,000 penalty applies per form per year (Treas. Reg. §1.6038A-1, from 2017 tax years), even with no income. — [Beancount (May 2026)](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/09/form-5472-foreign-owned-us-llc-disregarded-entity-25000-penalty-trap-guide); [Taxes for Expats](https://cdn.taxesforexpats.com/articles/foreign-business/form-5472-penalty.html)
- Wyoming annual report license tax: $60 or 0.0002 × Wyoming assets, whichever is greater, plus a registered agent. — [search summary of Wyoming sources](https://terms.law/Invest-USA/legal-compliance/form-5472.html)
- **Lei 14.754/2023** (in force 1 Jan 2024) ended deferral for foreign controlled entities of resident individuals. Profits are taxed annually at 15% in the DIRPF, using accounting standards. — [EY Tax Alert](https://assets.ey.com/content/dam/ey-sites/ey-com/pt_br/topics/tax-alert/ey-tax-alert-11-01-24.pdf); [Inter](https://acionista.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/3e1f403fc81341f09961b38153084682_inter-novas-regras-investimentos-exterior-dez23_compressed.pdf)
- The law describes transparency (income treated as the owner's, taxed annually at 15%) and opacity (taxed on distribution) regimes. — [Times Brasil](https://timesbrasil.com.br/minhas-financas/imposto-de-renda/lei-14-754-muda-tributacao-de-empresas-offshore-e-aplicacoes-no-exterior/)
- **SC Cosit 56/2026:** a US LLC with non-US-resident members that is tax-transparent is "obrigatoriamente um RFP" (privileged tax regime), based on art. 2, VII of IN RFB 1.037/2010. Consequence: transfer-pricing rules apply to transactions with such entities regardless of any related-party link, per the article. — [Pimentel & Rohenkohl, 30 Apr 2026](https://pradvogados.com.br/2026/04/30/a-classificacao-das-llcs-americanas-como-regime-fiscal-privilegiado-uma-analise-critica-da-solucao-de-consulta-cosit-no-56-26/)
- Profits of an LLC with a Brazil-resident individual member are taxed in the DIRPF on 31 Dec of the year, regardless of distribution (press summary). There is no Brazil–US double tax treaty, so credit for US tax is uncertain. — [Jornal do Brás](https://jornaldobras.com.br/noticia/131108/o-que-muda-para-brasileiros-que-investem-em-imoveis-nos-eua-apos-nova-interpretacao-da-receita-sobre-llcs); [TAGD Law](https://tagdlaw.com.br/fisco-vai-cobrar-ir-sobre-lucro-apurado-nos-eua/)

### Inferences
- **US side:** a US LLC selling inventory warehoused in the US to Brazil will likely have US-source ECI, since the business is conducted through US inventory and a 3PL. Under disregarded-entity treatment the foreign owner then files a 1040-NR, or the LLC could elect C-corp (21% federal tax). Do not assume a "zero US tax" LLC when inventory and staff are in the US. This is inference; I found no source.
- **Brazil side:** the combination of annual 15% taxation, RFP classification, transfer pricing on dealings between the Brazilian company and the LLC, and the CBC/DIRPF reporting duties makes a US LLC costly to run. It is justified mainly if the US entity must hold inventory, contract the 3PL and receive card or eFX settlement.
- **Banking:** many US banks and processors avoid CBD, and the 11 Dec 2026 change will tighten this further. Expect high-risk processors and possibly CBD-friendly credit unions or fintechs. I found no specific named banks with sources.
- Delaware vs Wyoming vs Florida: Florida may be logical if the 3PL is in Miami, because operating there would require foreign-qualifying anyway. This is inference only.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced formation or maintenance costs for Delaware or Florida.
- I did not read the original text of SC Cosit 56/2026. The press reports conflict on whether it concerns resident or non-resident members.
- I found no source naming banks that open accounts for foreign-owned CBD LLCs.
- Lei 14.596/2023 (new transfer pricing) applied to an RFP LLC was not covered by the sources found.
